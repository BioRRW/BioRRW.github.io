About Reed Woyda

Bridging the exact precision of a research scientist with the structural intuition of a home builder to create custom, heirloom-quality furniture in Fort Collins, Colorado.

The Foundation: Respect & Mastery

My foundation in the trades began from the ground up, working year-round through brutal Minnesota winters and intense summers. I started working with my father when I was 15 and continued, off and on, until I was finishing my undergraduate degrees. Over seven years with my father's company, Woyda Home Builders, I helped construct over a dozen custom homes, renovations, and agricultural builds.

My father taught me through example. He taught me the two most important lessons of my career: respect and mastery. He insisted that we go over the property twice—sweeping until the jobsite was cleaner than we found it—to ensure we left the project with the same care we would want in our own home. Later, when I started finish work, he would make me cut the same mitered baseboard joint over and over again until it was perfect. He taught me that repetition is the pathway to mastery and that this level of quality is what people deserve.

This early, rugged exposure forged my core belief: things should be built to take a beating. Just as a house must withstand the elements, furniture must withstand real life. A kitchen table should be sturdy enough to double as a kids' fort, and a hardwood dresser needs to survive decades of slammed drawers by a line of angsty teenagers.

The Science of Efficiency

My path to fine woodworking isn't traditional. Professionally, my background is rooted in the biological sciences and bioinformatics. I have spent years building high-quality, complex data pipelines designed to solve specific problems and make the lives of scientists easier and more efficient.

I apply that exact same scientific rigor to my woodshop. I believe furniture doesn't need to be overly fancy, but it absolutely must be efficient. Whether I am engineering a custom dining bench with hidden appliance storage to maximize a small kitchen, or designing a streamlined mudroom system so you can drop your gear frustration-free after a long day, my goal is to solve spatial problems. Woodworking allows me to merge the analytical, problem-solving mindset of a researcher with the tangible satisfaction of building something that makes your daily routine smoother.

Rejecting Disposable Culture

Today, my family and I call Fort Collins, Colorado home. I love this community because it shares my appreciation for sustainability, locally sourced materials, and handcrafted quality over mass production.

When you repeatedly buy from a mass producer, you are accepting that the item will end up broken on a local marketplace in just a few years. It might save money now, but it fails in the long term. When you commission a custom piece or hire me for architectural repair, you are investing in simplicity, comfort, and resale value. You are getting a craftsman who approaches your home with the structural understanding of a seasoned builder, the precision of a researcher, and an unwavering commitment to build pieces meant to outlast us.
